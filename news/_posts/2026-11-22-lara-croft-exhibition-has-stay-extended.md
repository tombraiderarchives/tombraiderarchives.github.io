A temporary exhibition showcasing the chronicles of Tomb Raider has proved so popular it will run for a year.

Treasures from the franchise went on display at Pickford's House in Derby to mark the 30-year anniversary of the birth of Lara Croft in July.

The museum is located a short walk from the studio in Ashbourne Road where game designers created the archaeologist-adventurer who would go on to conquer the world.

The free Lara Croft: 30 Years On exhibition, which features collectibles, costumes and rare artefacts including the computer used by Core Design to bring the legend to life, was initially set to run until November.

Now Derby City Council has announced the exhibition will stay on display until 31 July 2027.

A spokesperson for the authority said more than 10,000 people had already visited.

Visitors to Derby can also now view a 9ft (2.7m) Lara Croft statue.

The sculpture formed part of the Tomb Raider: The LIVE Experience in Camden Market in London but had been in storage since 2023.

After a £30,000 fundraising appeal it was brought to Derby to go on permanent display.

Once a plinth has been built the statue will be placed in the Market Hall, but until then it can be viewed at the Museum of Making.

Lara Croft has left a physical mark elsewhere in Derby too, with part of the city's inner ring road from Osmaston Road to Burton Road named Lara Croft Way in 2010.

A star in her name is also set into the pavement in Iron Gate as part of the city's Walk of Fame in 2018.
