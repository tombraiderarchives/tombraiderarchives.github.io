An exhibition hosted by Derby Museums celebrating Lara Croft and Tomb Raider has been extended until next summer after it proved to be a record-breaking success.

Lara Croft: 30 Years On – From Derby to Global Icon has attracted more than 10,000 visitors, many from overseas, since it opened at Pickfords’ House Museum in Friar Gate in July.

Featuring merchandise, mannequins and costumes, the exhibition is the first of its kind in the world.

It takes visitors through the 30-year history of the Tomb Raider franchise, starting with its creation by Derby video games firm Core Design in 1996.

The undoubted star of the show is the game’s central character, British archaeologist Lara Croft, who became a cultural icon after the first game’s release.

Her appeal, and the ongoing popularity of Tomb Raider, have brought more than 100 people to the exhibition on average every day, including actresses Shelley Blond and Judith Gibbins, who voiced Lara in the early games.

It also drew record numbers during the city’s recent Lara Croft Day, including hundreds of fans who were in Derby for the annual Tomb Raider Day event at QUAD, and it has also boosted sales at the Pickfords’ House shop, which had the most profitable day in its history last month thanks to a curated range of Tomb Raider merchandise.

These include special edition wooden Lara Croft standees made in the public workshop at the Museum of Making – which is also home to the city’s new nine-foot-tall Lara Croft statue, which has been given temporary residence there before it is transferred to the Market Hall later this year.

The exhibition, which replaced the successful Dracula Returns to Derby: 101 Years and Counting, was due to close at the end of this month, but Derby Museums, which runs the venue, has agreed to extend it until July 31 next year to meet the ongoing demand.

Dr Alex Rock, director of commercial and operations at Derby Museums, said: “The transformation of Pickfords’ House into Croft Manor has been a huge success, celebrating the incredible work of Core Design.

“We’re grateful for all who have supported. The visitor figures alongside the international Tomb Raider 30 visitors helped to make October the busiest month for Derby Museums since COVID and long may it continue.”

Around 400 of the items in the exhibition belong to Derby Tomb Raider collector Luke Earle, who has been a fan ever since he watched his dad play the first game in 1996.

He also organises the annual Tomb Raider Day, which marked the 30th anniversary this year and was a sell-out, with 400 people travelling to the city from all over the world.

He said: “The fact that more than 10,000 people have come to see the exhibition shows what a huge deal Lara Croft and Tomb Raider are, and I’m really happy that it has been extended until July next year.

“With the 30th anniversary, two new games and an Amazon TV series on the way, there’s never been a better time to celebrate Lara Croft’s legacy in the city that made her.”
