Amazon Games, Crystal Dynamics and Flying Wild Hog released a 30th anniversary Tomb Raider: Legacy of Atlantis trailer on October 25, 2026. Gematsu reported the October 25 release that day. The teaser runs one minute, according to itechguides. The game is a reimagining of the 1996 original that made Lara Croft famous.

It launches February 12, 2027, on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, Nintendo Switch 2 and PC via Steam. All versions arrive the same day, Notebookcheck reported on October 26. Thirty years on, Lara is heading back to the tombs that started it all. The question is how much of the old map survives the trip.

Fans saw the footage before the internet did. Notebookcheck reports it screened first at TR30 Derby and MCM London. Derby is a fitting first stop, since Core Design built the original game there, per Notebookcheck.

Under the hood, Dark Horizons reports the game runs on Unreal Engine 5 and adds skill trees. The globe-trotting is intact. Lara hunts pieces of the Scion, according to the PlayStation Store listing. The search spans Peru’s jungles, Greece’s ruins, Egypt’s deserts and a mysterious Mediterranean island.

This teaser builds on earlier footage. Notebookcheck notes an earlier trailer focused on reworked Greek levels. The anniversary teaser plays like a highlight reel of the whole expedition.

Here is the history lesson. Notebookcheck calls Legacy of Atlantis “a second reimagining of the 1996 game.” The first was Tomb Raider: Anniversary, Crystal Dynamics’ 2007 remake of the original. Per Notebookcheck, it rebuilt the 1996 game on an expanded version of the 2006 Tomb Raider: Legend engine.

This time the studios are reaching further. Dark Horizons describes an expanded Atlantis story that ties together eras of Lara Croft. That is a big swing. Lara has lived several lives across reboots, and her timelines rarely line up neatly.

The upside is real. A unified Lara could give new players one coherent hero instead of a stack of competing versions. The risk is just as clear. The 1996 game worked because it left so much unsaid. Atlantis was a mystery you uncovered in silence, and extra lore could fill that silence with explanation.

The wider remake wave shows what is at stake. The Crash Bandicoot N. Sane Trilogy proved how much goodwill a faithful rebuild can earn. Legacy of Atlantis is betting on reinvention instead. Lara has earned that gamble, given her place among iconic female game characters. The danger is a half-measure that pleases neither camp.

The wait got longer this year. The game originally targeted a 2026 window. Then it slipped. Notebookcheck reports the February 12, 2027, date was confirmed in June. According to itechguides, the reveal came during the June 2 State of Play.

Pricing is already set. Notebookcheck lists the standard edition at $59.99, with a Survivor Outfit as a pre-order bonus. The PlayStation Store listing shows a Deluxe Edition with the Parisian Fugitive Outfit and a DLC story pack.

Keep this separate from Tomb Raider: Catalyst. Dark Horizons reports that new adventure is due in 2028. So Lara has two games on the calendar, one looking back and one looking forward.

Then there is the rumor mill. Dark Horizons flags unconfirmed talk of Tomb Raider II (1997) and Tomb Raider III (1998) remakes. Those could follow if this one does well. Treat that as rumor until someone official confirms it.

I went into the Tomb Raider: Legacy of Atlantis trailer skeptical. Remaking the same game twice in two decades felt like a franchise running in circles. One minute of footage changed my mood, if not my mind.

Bullet time suits Lara’s old-school bravado, and the Scion hunt still looks like the right spine. The Atlantis story is what matters most. If tying her eras together adds weight to that hunt, this could become the definitive version of where Lara began. If it buries the mystery under lore, Anniversary remains the better time capsule.

February 12 will tell us which.
