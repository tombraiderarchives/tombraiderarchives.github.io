After a tumultuous PS3 era that divided players, while still producing some incredible games that pushed the console to its limits, Sony went to great lengths to ensure that their next home console was friendly to both players and game developers. The result was the PlayStation 4, which was both hugely successful and widely acclaimed. Thanks to using the popular x86 architecture as opposed to the PS3's infamous Cell Processor, it was relatively easy to develop games for the PS4, insofar as developing games can ever be called "easy."

The PS4 may not have been a graphical leap above the previous generation the way the PS3 was a leap over the PS2, but framerates became smoother, resolutions got a boost, and the introduction of HDR color-grading allowed for the PS4 to have some truly incredible-looking games.

Let's take a look at five particularly gorgeous PS4 titles that showed off the power of the console. These games feature grand vistas, stunning animation, and boundary-pushing art styles. Simply put, these games would have been absolutely impossible on the PS3. One thing to keep in mind: some of these games would get remastered for PS5 or otherwise run better on Sony's current generation of hardware, but we're only looking at the PS4 and PS4 Pro versions for this story.

In 2013, the long-running "Tomb Raider" franchise was rebooted for the second time with a brand new story about a young Lara Croft learning how to become a survivor. This story continued with 2015's "Rise of the Tomb Raider" before concluding with "Shadow of the Tomb Raider" in 2018. For the first time in the trilogy, there was no version for Xbox 360, which means "Shadow" was finally a current-gen exclusive.

"Shadow of the Tomb Raider" is largely set in a Peruvian jungle, with lush foliage, mud to wade around in, and beautiful lakes in which to swim around. This might not seem like a massive leap beyond what was seen in previous titles, particularly the gorgeous winter landscape of "Rise of the Tomb Raider," but "Shadow of the Tomb Raider" has a secret weapon: HDR color grading. While some games are subtle or even lazy with their HDR implementation, "Shadow of the Tomb Raider" is an absolute smokeshow in HDR, so to speak.

If you're playing "Shadow of the Tomb Raider" on a regular ol' SDR television, you haven't had the full "Tomb Raider" experience. If you don't understand what the fuss is about HDR, "Shadow of the Tomb Raider" will make you a believer.

Generally speaking, open world games feature less in-depth detail and overall visual fidelity than their more linear counterparts. Linear games have more narrow play spaces to explore, so they can afford to invest more detail in these limited spaces. On the other hand, open world games are huge, so there's less expectation for the developers to fill every inch of the land with outstanding sights. Basically, linear games favor visual quality, while open world games favor quantity.

When it comes to Rockstar Games, though, they have the budget for both. While 2013's "Grand Theft Auto V" is an all-time great game, it was originally released for PlayStation 3, and that's obvious, even on the PS4 and PS5 versions. For this story, we're looking at "Red Dead Redemption II," released in 2018. The game features a representation of a facsimile of 1800s America, from the New Orleans-esque Saint Denis to the Rocky Mountains-adjacent Grizzlies region. These locations, and everything in between, are beautiful and detailed like nothing you've ever seen in an open world game.

But they're not just empty wilderness. There is so much to see and do in these areas, you can practically smell the flowers in real life as you stroll through a field on horseback.
