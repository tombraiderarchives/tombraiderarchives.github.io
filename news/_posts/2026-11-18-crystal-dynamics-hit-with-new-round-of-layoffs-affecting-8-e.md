While Crystal Dynamics continues its work on the upcoming title Tomb Raider: Legacy of Atlantis, a spokesperson for the studio has confirmed that it has undergone layoffs affecting eight of its employees. In a statement, according to Vertical Slice, the spokesperson called this decision “a difficult but necessary” one despite how its game production pipeline currently works.

Along with this, the spokesperson has also confirmed that those affected by the layoffs will get full severance packages and help with job placement.

Confirmation of the layoffs came following some of the studio’s now-former workers taking to LinkedIn to reveal that they had been affected. This includes a UI software engineer, among other members of the development team, who referred to it as “a reduction in staff.”

In his post, Joshua Mitchell even referenced the other rounds of layoffs that have hit Crystal Dynamics over the years, and mentioned that he got to work on a variety of projects, including the canceled Perfect Dark and the upcoming Tomb Raider: Catalyst.

“After several rounds of layoffs at Crystal Dynamics, today it was my turn. My first layoff in 14 years of making games. I got to work on Perfect Dark, Tomb Raider: Legacy of Atlantis, and Tomb Raider: Catalyst. I loved every minute of it, and I’m grateful to everyone I got to build alongside. I’m going to miss this team.”

The studio’s next project, Tomb Raider: Legacy of Atlantis, is slated for release across PC, PS5, Xbox Series X/S, and Nintendo Switch 2 on February 12th. Recently, the title’s experience director, Jeff Adams, spoke about how the remake of the original Tomb Raider isn’t simply a remaster made for fans of the classic. Rather, he said that it will be a way for newcomers to the franchise who got into it with more recent releases to experience the more outlandish adventures from the older games.

Adams also spoke about how the team has been paying attention to aspects of the game that truly make it Tomb Raider, like combat that “puts you up against apex predators that will try to make you their next meal.”

“As game developers, we bring our experience forward in how we construct our game. So, while we love Tomb Raider 1996 and Anniversary, we also understand there’s value in adding updated systems that give players more choice and more tunability in how they play. Providing a system that lets them bolster Lara’s attributes in ways that suit their tastes is a benefit.
